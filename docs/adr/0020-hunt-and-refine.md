# Hunt the core behind every failure and refine every core to its mark

A ruleset-3 session ran 21.2 hours with 10 crash reboots yet earned no clean guard rotation. Isolated checking took 12.8 hours; guard repeatedly re-ran R2 before reaching later regimes. Unattributed crashes on one CCD backed off eight cores together, with no observed improvement after eight counts. Attributed resident errors on a core also recurred at offsets that had passed isolated trials. Blaming every loaded core and automatically retrying lost depth neither located the failure nor maximized the depth the evidence allowed ([issue #64](https://github.com/shgew/togi/issues/64)).

## Decision

- Ruleset 4 keeps isolated search but checks each candidate edge with n passing R1 and R2 starts before the core moves directly to resident or done. It removes the separate isolated checking phase, load-scoped group backoff and automatic depth recovery. A start is one trial; n is `ceil(ln(evidence.miss)/log1p(-evidence.rate))`, five at the defaults. The ledger keys conclusive evidence by regime, workload, loaded cores and duration; deeper passing profiles cover shallower profiles, subject to intervening shallow failures.
- A clean guard rotation qualifies only when it covers three R1, three R2 and three R7 workload steps and R3–R6 at least once. R7 runs three short starts and a longer heat-soak trial on each loaded part. A backoff keeps rotation evidence valid where monotonicity permits; it reruns the failed class first at short and, if different, original duration. An unattributed resident failure instead starts a journaled hunt.
- The hunt chooses the newest valid strictly shallower qualified anchor or all-zero. Masks keep the failed workload and loaded cores; selected candidates take their failing offsets and others their anchor offsets. Delta debugging tests parts, complements and finer splits, with n starts per mask. An initial split that passes triggers one full-profile short check and escalation to the failed trial's duration when necessary. Direct attribution takes precedence, including a core held at its anchor offset. A singleton records a failed mark; multiple cores record a joint mark, explicitly tagged fallback when no mask reproduces the failure. Before a reproduced joint is marked, edge probes move each member in turn 1, 2, 4, … counts shallower, holding the members already probed at their shallowest failures, then bisect; the mark records every member's shallowest failing offset, so moving any one member a count shallower has passed. The joint's backoff member maximizes total reachable depth under all marks; preferred-core ranking breaks ties. Hunt events record the plan so it resumes exactly once across interruptions.
- Refinement targets the globally most-deep legal profile, maximizing total depth across every core and joint mark; preferred-core ranking breaks equal-sum ties. To reach it, a round may *yield* by moving one core shallower and move others halfway deeper. It checks deepened cores on R1/R2 and affected R7 parts with n starts per class. Done means −50 or one deeper count reaching a mark. Bronze requires all cores done, no further global improvement and a clean qualifying rotation since last deepening. Silver and Gold use a tier clock that restarts on a failure at least as deep as the current profile, not merely on a profile change.
- A recorded signal or machine check precedes reset reason in crash classification. Without it, thermal trip is a dead end; holding the power button during a trial counts as failure but outside a trial is inconclusive. A missing reason is power loss only when supported and reporting has been confirmed in the session. Monotonic timestamps select machine checks even across wall-clock jumps. Backend/kernel-log failures are retried after 1, 5 and 30 minutes, while missing binaries stop immediately. A journal write error triggers emergency zeroing and readback, without a new journal intent. The tuning boot's restart-limit hook clears the saved GRUB entry and reboots to the normal system, except for dead-end exits.
- Ruleset changes archive and seed a session as in [ADR 0019](0019-a-ruleset-change-starts-a-seeded-session.md). A *same-ruleset BIOS context change* now also archives and starts a seeded session with candidate edges but no old failed marks. A hunt culprit's failed mark can carry on a same-BIOS ruleset transition; joint marks cannot. A core reset clears its failed mark and all joint marks naming it.

This supersedes [ADR 0011](0011-blame-by-load-and-automatic-regain.md)'s blame and depth-retry strategy, [ADR 0013](0013-candidate-edges-for-a-new-session.md)'s candidate-edge checking phase, [ADR 0015](0015-avx-512-first-in-confirmation.md)'s ordering of that phase, and the same-ruleset BIOS dead end in [ADR 0019](0019-a-ruleset-change-starts-a-seeded-session.md). Journal schema remains 2; the older ruleset's journal is archived, not reinterpreted in place.

## Considered Options

- **Keep blaming the loaded scope and retrying one count at a clean rotation:** rejected because unrelated cores lose depth together, repeated failures need not narrow the culprit and retesting all lost depth together can mask the failure behind a later group backoff.
- **Single-culprit binary splitting:** rejected because interacting cores may fail together even when either half passes. Delta debugging with complements records both a set and the limits of its evidence.
- **Noisy Bayesian group testing:** deferred because its failure model adds complexity; reconsider if hunts routinely need more than eight masks or end without a culprit.
- **Back a joint off one count per hunt:** rejected because a joint that holds well above its first failure then costs one full hunt, crash included, per count, spread across its members. On one simulated three-core joint over seeds 1–8, this took up to 79 hunts and 359 crashes; with edge probes, at most 8 hunts and 97 crashes, and every seed ended at least as deep.
- **Probe only the member the backoff rule picks:** rejected because the other coordinates of the mark stay at their first failure, so refinement brings the probed member back deep with another member one count shallower and the joint fails again; on the same simulated joint this still recorded 17 joint marks. Probing every member costs extra masks when the first failure already sits on one member's edge: one such seed needed 15 more masks and 11 more crashes for the same profile.
- **Merge a joint's marks into one at each member's shallowest failing offset:** rejected because it forbids profiles never seen to fail, a margin the observed-edge rule avoids, and without probing it still moves one count per failure.
- **Stop once every core is locally done:** rejected because interacting joint marks allow equal or deeper *global* profiles reachable only by yielding a core. Ranking is a tie breaker, not a substitute for optimizing total depth.
- **Keep the same-ruleset BIOS dead end until manual reset:** rejected because a context change invalidates marks but archived edges still provide useful candidate starts; carrying old marks would be unsafe.

## Consequences

- A failed trial may lead to many masked starts before a mark is recorded, and a fallback joint mark is cautious rather than proof that every member caused the failure. Starts, classes and masks are journaled to expose that distinction.
- Short breadth coverage catches frequent failure; rare failures need guard exposure. Carried passes can satisfy coverage after a backoff but cannot make the tier clock's zero-failure bound optimistic.
- A search or refinement move may deepen a core, but a mark-aware checked profile writer prevents even a transient intermediate SMU profile from reaching a recorded mark. Interrupted hunts and rounds need idempotent recorded commitments.
- Ruleset-3 journals transition once to ruleset 4. A changed BIOS transitions with edges only. Operators can still use `reset --core` to clear a mistaken mark or `reset --all` to discard all carry.
