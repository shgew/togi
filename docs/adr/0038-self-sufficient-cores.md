# Self-sufficient cores under shared voltage

Status: **Accepted**.

Amended by [ADR 0040](0040-located-hunts.md): an unattributed multi-core R7 failure with an unloaded core off CO 0 is first located by a hunt that holds the loaded cores at their failing offsets, the `failure_at_zero` dead end holds regardless of idle cores only after that hunt keeps the failure on the loaded cores and the failing trial also failed with every core at CO 0, and the Ruleset 10 gate, as the owner amended it on 2026-10-07, scores all 24 seeds per scenario pooled on the median and 90th-percentile worst R7 hazard instead of the median and maximum, exempts #107's default-cycle cost from the 2× time bound, and also covers the hand-set scenarios `default`, `idle-limit` and `late-onset`.

Supersedes [ADR 0023](0023-hunts-that-converge-on-shared-voltage.md) and the parts of [ADR 0020](0020-hunt-and-refine.md) about multi-core R7 hunts, joint/combination backoff and deferred noisy group testing. Their hunt mechanics remain available outside multi-core R7; R1–R6, R6 idle failures, single-core failures and their first-failure rules are unchanged. [Issue #105](https://github.com/shgew/togi/issues/105) records the approved decision; [#306](https://github.com/shgew/togi/issues/306) records the simulator prerequisites.

## Context

The two CCDs share VDDCR: the highest loaded-core voltage request sets the voltage. Offsets do not rank requests across cores: measured per-core bases spread about 47 mV on CCD0 and 28 mV on CCD1, equivalent to roughly 13 and 8 offset counts. Parking a core at zero can rescue the load and changes clocks, so a passing hunt group need not clear its members under the failing load. In ruleset 8, the CCD1 partial without core 10 failed four of four AVX-512 trials with computation errors on core 11 while its full part passed four of four. Of 62 ruleset-7 R7 failures, 43 crashed before 20 samples; the failing trial often cannot measure its own request order.

ADR 0023 rejected “back off the member that sets the voltage, the shallowest one” as an unmeasured hardware explanation. We now choose the measured top requester, not the shallowest offset. Partial loads test the cores that its request otherwise protects.

## Decision

Ruleset 9 aims for every core to be **self-sufficient** under each R7 workload: passing as a **top requester**. Cores within 1 mV of the highest request tie. For each CCD, checking runs the full part, then a chain idling successive top groups while at least two cores remain loaded; all-core runs last. A one-CCD machine runs its full/all-core part once before its chain. Each part requires three short and one long passing trials and contributes ordinary evidence and full-cycle coverage. Ruleset-8 record-only facts become ordinary evidence after the transition.

Requests come from the latest measurement of exactly the workload and loaded set, shifted to the current profile at 3.6 mV per count. An unmeasured partial uses the CCD's latest measured full part, restricted to its cores. Only absent telemetry permits offset order. Each derivation cites its source or explains that fallback. Derive a partial after its predecessor completes, freeze its set once started across resume, and re-derive unstarted parts after a profile change.

A named multi-core R7 failure counts against the named core. Otherwise count it against each affected CCD's top group; a persisted `stalled_core` restricts an unattributed all-core failure to that CCD without naming its culprit. There are no multi-core R7 hunts, parked groups, member probes or new combinations. R1–R6, R6 idle and single-core failures retain their hunts and first-failure rules.

Every multi-core R7 failure requires a move unless the core it would move already sits shallower than its offset in the failed trial; such a failure records no decision and stays pending until that core returns to its failing offset or deeper. If an unattributed failure's top group is already at CO 0, select the next group containing a movable loaded core in the same CCD's request order. An all-core failure skips an affected CCD whose loaded cores were all at 0 in that trial when another affected CCD has a movable loaded core. The `failure_at_zero` dead end occurs only when no affected CCD has a movable loaded core, regardless of idle cores; a `stalled_core` CCD at 0 dead-ends regardless of another CCD. A named core already at 0 dead-ends only if it was a top requester in that trial; otherwise route to its CCD's top group, including stepdown when necessary, or, when its CCD had no loaded core, count the failure as unattributed against the loaded CCD's top group.

Use the trial's recorded top requesters, or derive them from measurements available as of that trial, not later observations. For CO-0 top-group stepdown with a measured request for the moved core, take the greater of the ordinary voltage-targeted count and `floor((top_request - selected_request) / 3.6 mV) + 1`, aiming above the current top. Clamp at CO 0; reaching that offset need not reach the desired voltage. When an offset stands in for the moved core's request, even beside other cores' measurements, step down using offset order and apply the ordinary one-count fallback; decisions explain the fallback rather than invent voltages or claim a physical request above the top.

The self-sufficiency ledger remains per core, workload and intended duration, counting top-requester passing trials at equal-or-deeper offsets and failures at equal-or-shallower offsets where the core was top or the failure named it. It supports self-sufficiency evidence and voltage targets, never permission to tolerate a failure. Same-BIOS carried facts count without aging and are evaluated before the first trial. Known R7 failures are processed once, not rerun as skips or counted twice.

Backoff targets measured passing voltage rather than an arbitrary stride. For an unattributed failure, move one movable core in the selected request group, lowest-ranked first, toward the lowest higher top request where the same load passed n trials. A named core moves toward the lowest top request it passed under in any load of that workload at an equal or higher CCD clock (no clock filter without failing clock telemetry). Counts round up at 3.6 mV per count from the moved core's offset in the failed trial, at least one; no qualifying pass, or an offset standing in for the moved core's own request, means one count. Record the moved core's failure point and ordinary rerun obligations, citing the failure, request measurements and qualifying passes. Deepening rechecks R1/R2 alone, the core's top-requester partial for each R7 workload and the full CCD part when it is or becomes a top requester; re-order the chain when its rank changes.

## Considered options

- **Offset-based m-th-deepest rule:** rejected because offsets misrank measured voltage requests across cores.
- **Keep hunts with idled cores:** rejected because changing the loaded set changes clocks and voltage; hunt passes do not answer the original condition.
- **One partial per CCD:** rejected because the next requester can protect further cores; the chain tests them in turn.
- **Per-class failure-rate bound:** rejected; the per-core/workload/duration ledger describes self-sufficiency across loaded sets, but no failure-rate bound controls moves.
- **Always back off one count:** rejected because measured passing voltage supplies a justified multi-count target; one count remains the fallback without a qualifying pass.
- **5% failure-rate tolerance at significance 0.2:** rejected on 2026-10-04. On the hand-set shared-voltage machine it raised worst R7 hazard from 0.47 to 1.74 failures/h and bought neither depth nor time ([#386](https://github.com/shgew/togi/issues/386)). No failure is tolerated; conclusion remains one clean cycle when requested.
- **Noisy group testing:** not pursued for multi-core R7 because the measured shared-voltage mechanism and partial chain replace those hunts rather than improve their group-test inference.

## Consequences

The ruleset bump archives the ruleset-8 session and seeds a new session from compatible carried facts; operators need not reset or transcribe them. Decisions expose request sources, chain derivations, same-CCD CO-0 stepdown and voltage targets. A clean cycle is still breadth of tested workloads, not proof against rare or delayed failures. The existing `evidence.miss` and `evidence.rate` pass-count rule remains for hunts and other required replicated checks; R7 has no tolerance configuration.

A shared-voltage fit used for forward claims or to regenerate `target-fit-*` must beat the constant on held-out-session R7 log loss and predict total failures within a factor of two. The first candidate failed on 2026-10-04: 36.0 predicted against 17, and the check was not blind ([#306](https://github.com/shgew/togi/issues/306)). That failure is not a forward-validation claim.

Evaluation instead requires an in-sample all-facts shared-voltage anchor, committed as `target-shared-voltage.toml`, explicitly labelled not forward-validated, passing the model check on every eligible group. Adversaries derived from it must reproduce ruleset 8's real failure modes. If no in-sample fit passes, use hand-set adversaries explicitly labelled as having no anchor.

The gate compares ruleset 9 against ruleset 8 on every shared-voltage scenario: each adversary and the hand-set `shared-voltage.toml`, with 12 dev and 12 holdout seeds each. Ruleset 9 must conclude wherever ruleset 8 did; its final-profile worst R7 hazard's median and maximum must be no higher; and its time must be at most twice ruleset 8's. Across adversaries pooled, median worst R7 hazard must be strictly lower. Legacy scenarios, crashes and depth are reported, not gated. If no anchored adversary makes ruleset 8 worse, or the gate fails, stop for the owner's decision.

Rework ruleset 9 before any adversary exists. Search adversaries against ruleset 8 only; score ruleset 9 once on dev, then confirm once on holdout. After the dev score, changes are defect fixes with tests, not tuning to the scenarios. If the seeded default simulator machine still cannot reach a clean cycle, `just sim` defaults to `shared-voltage.toml`; the bench retains the default machine.
